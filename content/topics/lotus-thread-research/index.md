Over the last decade or so, there have been multiple parties who have
studied the economics around lotus silk, in Burma and beyond. Listed
here are various studies of lotus silk. The first one is the most
germane and gets down to wages for various jobs, identifying where
value is added. The single most interesting infoid is that the labor
of extracting and spinning the thread dominiates the labor costs --
somewhere between roughly 60% and 90% of the labor cost.

## Microfiber

There are multiple claims backed up with things like electron microspopy backing up the claims
that lotus fiber is a natural microfiber.

### Lampoon article

- [Lampoon article](https://lampoonmagazine.com/lotus-fiber-natural-microfiber-samatoa-cambodia/)
  > In 2016, samples were sent to the French Institute of Textile and Clothing for analysis. The results identified lotus fiber as the first known natural microfiber in the world. Microfibers are defined as fibers with a linear density below one dtex—less than one gram per ten thousand meters of yarn. Conventionally, microfibers are synthetic, typically made from polyester or polyamide. Lotus fiber, by contrast, is entirely natural.

## Flood mitigation

In 2023 Vietnam started a three year pilot to see what lotus could do to mitigate flooding of the Mekong, while also providing a stock of lotus stems for weaving, amongst other things.

- [Kick-off workshop on lotus silk value chains to expand flood storage in the Mekong Delta](https://iucn.org/story/202307/kick-workshop-lotus-silk-value-chains-expand-flood-storage-mekong-delta)
- [Lotus fiber: a new women-led flood friendly value chain in the Mekong Delta](https://iucn.org/story/202407/lotus-fiber-new-women-led-flood-friendly-value-chain-mekong-delta)
